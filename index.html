ResearchMarket microstructure

Market making on a prediction-market order book

Implementing and calibrating a quoting model for Polymarket's central limit order book, starting with traditional sports markets. The model is an Avellaneda–Stoikov-style framework adapted to a binary payoff, working in logit space. With about $100 of capital the dollar PnL is statistical noise by design, so the deliverable is the model, its calibration against live data, and the risk controls.

  • Where equity analogies break: jumps near resolution and bounded prices change the inventory and quoting math.
  • Scoping from data: esports were excluded after live data showed prices moving roughly 10–30× faster per minute than baseball markets, so the reaction parameters did not transfer.
  • Research log: API and fee research, every decision dated with its reason, claims backed by a derivation, a citation or a test.
  • Hard rules: a backtest on a resolved market before risking capital; no live order without explicit confirmation.
  • Earlier result: an event study of public figures' posts against market prices found no capturable signal; it was closed and kept as a reference.

Python · Stochastic control · Backtesting · pytest · CLOB API

How I work

Skeptical by default

A suspiciously good backtest is a methodology bug until proven otherwise: look-ahead, overfitting, fees and slippage.

Assumptions stated

Every number comes with the data, window and conditions it is valid under, and what would break it.

Tests where it matters

Math, fee calculations, position sizing and anything that touches money get tests before they are called done.

Humans in the loop

Automation answers what it can verify and escalates the rest. Nothing irreversible runs without confirmation.
