Systematic crypto strategy research

A pipeline for downloading data, backtesting and falsifying strategies on perpetual futures and options. Backtests charge trading fees, and strategies with fitted parameters get a walk-forward split. Most ideas did not survive, and that is documented.

StrategyFinding
Cointegrated pairs55 of 190 pairs significant in the screen, but every half-life is above 240 h. Too slow to trade.
Basis / funding20 symbols, mean Sharpe 5.3 but only 4.8% annualized (2.3% out of sample, 17 of 20 symbols positive). Sound, low return.
Lead–lag (BTC → alts)No significant lead–lag for any of 10 alts on 5-minute data. Discarded.
Cluster reversion0 of 10 symbols profitable after costs. Discarded.
Variance risk premiumSimplified short-straddle proxy on Deribit: Sharpe 1.9 (BTC), 0.6 (ETH). Exploratory.

Shows: experiment design, walk-forward validation, stating caveats (full-sample screen, no multiple-testing correction, simplified P&L), honest reporting of negative results.

Market making on a prediction-market order book

Does a quoting model adapted to a binary payoff earn an edge on Polymarket's order book? I tested it with simulated quoting against the live book and against recorded tape. It does not, and the repository documents why.

  • Result: with risk protections, across 50 events grouped by event, mean PnL was negative in every run (t ≈ −3.4).
  • What I tried: wider spreads, a toxic-flow detector calibrated on 48,446 real trades, and skewing quotes with the flow. None fixed it; the skew made it 32% worse.
  • Why: a binary market converges to a known outcome, so inventory that is not closed in time pays the full move. The equity market-making analogy breaks here.
  • Earlier work: an event study of public figures' posts and supply-chain theses against market prices, also closed with a null result.

Shows: stochastic modeling, hypothesis testing with correct units of analysis, data ingestion, 140 tests, and reporting a negative result.

Customer-service automation and CRM for a used-vehicle dealership

End-to-end system for a small dealership: an LLM-powered WhatsApp assistant that answers questions from live inventory, a private admin CRM, a public inventory website and an Instagram agent. Whatever the assistant cannot resolve is escalated to the owner.

  • Assistant: Meta webhook, FastAPI, Claude with tool use (inventory search, spec lookup, vehicle history report, photos, escalation). Specs come from search, not model memory.
  • Data: PostgreSQL with inventory, leads and conversations; hourly inventory sync from a spreadsheet; weekly snapshots of US and local market prices to compute resale margins.
  • Scheduled work: follow-ups after 24 h, 72 h and 7 days of silence, appointment reminders.
  • Surfaces: React admin CRM over a private VPN, Next.js public website with financing calculator, SEO and analytics.
  • Media: FFmpeg and Whisper for automatic video editing and captions. Instagram agent for comment moderation and DMs.
  • Guardrails: message de-duplication, spam and vendor detection, image understanding for documents, owner escalation.

Shows: LLM tool use in production, data modeling in SQL, scheduled pipelines, deployment and monitoring.
